# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms used throughout the rules. Any **bold
keyword** in a chapter links here — click it to read the definition without
leaving the page.

## Dice & Rolls

**Die Size**: The number of sides on every die. All dice in the game share the same Die Size, so a result is always a number from 1 up to the Die Size.

**Roll**: A single handful of dice thrown against a Target Number. Everything in the game — attacks, saves, skills — is built out of Rolls.

**Target Number**: The number each die must reach to count as a success. The lower your Target Number, the easier the Roll. Often shortened to TN.

**Base Target Number**: The Target Number a Check starts from before any bonuses or penalties are applied.

**Minimum Target Number**: The lowest a Target Number can ever be set to, no matter how many bonuses pile up.

**Maximum Target Number**: The highest a Target Number can ever be set to, no matter how many penalties pile up.

**Critical Success**: A die that rolls the highest possible result. It usually counts as two successes instead of one.

## Checks

**Check**: An action that can fail and so calls for dice — swinging a sword, picking a lock, recalling a rumor, resisting a poison. Every Check is made of one or more Rolls.

**Dice Cap**: The number of dice you roll for a Check, set by the most relevant attribute and your Prowess. More dice mean more chances to succeed.

**Minimum Dice**: The fewest dice any Check rolls — the floor your Dice Cap resets to once it passes the Maximum Dice.

**Maximum Dice**: The most dice any Check rolls — the ceiling your Dice Cap reaches before it resets back to the Minimum Dice.

**Prowess**: A measure of how practiced and capable a creature is. Higher Prowess raises the Dice Cap, letting you roll more dice.

**Attribute**: A creature's core scores (such as Might or Wits) that describe its raw capabilities. The most relevant attribute to a Check helps set its Dice Cap.

**Attribute Check**: The most basic Check: a raw test of an attribute for a task that training doesn't help with, like shoving a boulder or remembering an overheard name.

**Aptitude Check**: A Check for a trained skill, where practice and know-how improve your odds.

**Saving Throw**: A roll made to resist or avoid magical or mundane effects such as compulsions, explosions, or entangling vines. Also includes recognizing illusions by their inconsistencies.

**Opposed Aptitude Check**: A Check where two creatures pit the same kind of skill directly against each other, like an arm-wrestle or a battle of wits.

**Single-Target Magic Check**: A Check to land a spell or magical ability on one creature — the caster's casting roll against the target's Saving Throw.

**Multi-Target Magic Check**: A Check to land a spell or magical ability on several named creatures at once — one casting roll against each target's own Saving Throw.

**Area Magic Check**: A Check to land a spell or magical ability on every creature in a defined area — one casting roll against each caught creature's Saving Throw.

**Combat Check**: A Check to hit another creature in combat with a weapon — an attack roll against the target's defense.

**Magic Combat Check**: A Combat Check whose attack is a spell or magical ability rather than a weapon — a casting roll against the target's defense.

**Competency Bonus**: A bonus a creature adds to a Check it is genuinely skilled at, reflecting its training and Tier.

## Bonuses & Penalties

**Bonus**: A positive modifier that makes a Roll easier by lowering its Target Number.

**Penalty**: A negative modifier that makes a Roll harder by raising its Target Number.

**Bonus Type**: The category a Bonus or Penalty belongs to (Circumstance, Guidance, Equipment, and so on). Bonuses and Penalties of the same type do not stack — only the single largest Bonus and the single harshest Penalty of each type count.

**Dice Modifier**: The combined effect of all the Bonuses and Penalties on a Check, after same-type stacking is settled: add the best Bonus of each type and subtract the worst Penalty of each type.

**Starting Value**: An adjustment carried into a Roll's score when the Bonuses or Penalties would have pushed the Target Number past its minimum or maximum. It preserves the part of a modifier that the Target Number alone couldn't hold.

**Starting Successes**: Successes added to a Roll's score before any dice are rolled — the positive side of a Starting Value, granted (for example) when a rise in attribute or Prowess resets your Dice Cap back to the Minimum Dice.

## Outcomes

**Degree of Success**: How well a Check turned out — the Supporting side's total successes minus the Opposing side's. The bigger the number, the better the result.

**Degree of Failure**: How badly a failed Check turned out — the size of a negative Degree of Success. Things that scale with how badly you missed read this number.

**Check Outcome**: The verdict of a Check, read from its Degree of Success: Success, Failure, or Fumble.

**Success**: A Check whose Degree of Success reaches the success threshold.

**Failure**: A Check that neither succeeds nor fumbles — including one that lands exactly at zero.

**Fumble**: A Check that fails badly enough to backfire. A Check can always Fumble.

## Power & Tier

**Tier**: A measure of magical power and density for a creature, object, condition, or ability. The higher the Tier, the stronger it is. Tier 0 counts as one-half in the math.

**Inherent**: The bonus that comes purely from a creature's Tier — its raw power, rather than any training or circumstance.

**Ascendancy**: The extra edge a creature gets when it badly out-powers its opponent. When two sides of a Check are not in the same league, Ascendancy amplifies the gap, helping the stronger and hurting the weaker.

## Multiple Participants

**Supporting Roll**: A Roll made by someone trying to make a Check succeed. There is always at least one.

**Opposing Roll**: A Roll made by someone trying to make a Check fail. There may be none.

**Initiating Roll**: The first Supporting Roll — the creature actually attempting the action.

**Defending Roll**: The first Opposing Roll, when there is one — the primary target of the action.

**Spread Check**: A Check against several targets at once (an area effect), where the acting side's result is measured against each caught creature separately, giving one Outcome per target.
